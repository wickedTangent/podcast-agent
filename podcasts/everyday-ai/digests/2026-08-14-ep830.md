## Topics Covered

### Cerebras Partnership: Near-Instant AI Inference

OpenAI announced a partnership with Cerebras to deploy GPT-5.3-Codex-Spark, a coding model running on Cerebras Wafer-Scale Engines at over 1,000 tokens per second -- roughly 15-20x faster than current models. This is OpenAI's first major inference partnership outside its traditional Nvidia infrastructure. The model is designed for real-time software development where responsiveness matters as much as intelligence. Cerebras is also adding 750MW of ultra low-latency AI compute to OpenAI's platform, using a single giant chip that eliminates the bottlenecks that slow conventional inference hardware.

### Workers Asking AI Agents Instead of Humans

Adobe surveyed 1,000+ American workers who use AI weekly and found that 68% would ask an AI agent an obvious question at work, while only 4% would ask their direct manager -- making workers 17 times more likely to ask AI than leadership. Workers chose agents because they answer instantly and never make them feel unprepared. Once workers trust an agent's output, they increasingly bypass both managers and specialists, building work in silos rather than through intentional collaboration. This shift erodes human connection and mentorship opportunities that traditionally came through those handoffs.

### GPT-5.6 and Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI)

OpenAI released GPT-5.6 (Sol, Terra, Luna models), with GPT-5.6 Sol scoring 16.2 points higher than GPT-5.5 on OpenAI's internal Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) benchmark. Sol autonomously identified training configurations, selected GPUs, and executed post-training scripts to train the smaller Luna model from a brief prompt -- saving an estimated two researchers about two weeks. This represents a real demonstration of AI improving AI, with Sol scoring 68.3% on Internal Research Debugging vs. 50% for GPT-5.5. OpenAI published the actual prompt used by a researcher to get Sol to train Luna, demonstrating transparency around this capability.

- [GPT-5.6: Frontier intelligence that scales with your ambition](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-6/) - OpenAI's official GPT-5.6 announcement with benchmark table

### AI Pacing Letter: "Pacing the Frontier"

Over 1,386 employees from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Meta signed an open letter titled "Pacing the Frontier," requesting that the U.S. government support development of technical and governance tools to deliberately pace the frontier of automated AI development. The letter warns that AI companies are close to automating AI research, and capability development could rapidly accelerate beyond human understanding or control. The signatories include senior staff members and some co-founders at these companies. Dario Amodei (Anthropic co-founder) published a companion essay titled "We Must Pace the Frontier."

### AI Connection Deficit: Productivity Gains vs. Human Erosion

Workday released global research (January 2026, surveying 2,150 workers) finding that 86% of employees feel more productive because of AI, and 62% report decreased burnout risk. However, the research also found a deepening "connection deficit": workers' conversations with colleagues rarely move past transactional work needs. Nearly 40% of time AI is supposed to save is spent reviewing, fixing, and reworking AI outputs. A separate March 2026 survey by Resume Now (1,000+ U.S. workers) found that 63% expect AI to make the workplace feel less human, with 42% citing dehumanization as a major concern. Gen Z workers are most at risk, with 20% taking time off due to loneliness or isolation.

### Middle Management Transformation: Everyone Becomes a Manager of Agents

Jordan argues that knowledge workers are becoming "middle managers of agents" -- directing dozens of AI agents simultaneously. He reports getting approximately 30 hours of equivalent work done daily as a single full-time employee. OpenAI staff at the 99th percentile generated over 60 hours of agent turns per day. The prediction: the middle management tier will get bigger and thicker, with fewer entry-level positions and fewer VP/C-suite roles. Most knowledge work will happen in this new middle layer where humans manage agents rather than people.

- [Introducing GPT-5 for developers](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-5-for-developers/) - OpenAI's GPT-5 developer release (coding benchmarks: 74.9% SWE-bench Verified, 88% Aider polyglot)

### Expert-Driven Loops vs. Passive Human-in-the-Loop

Jordan's framework argues that generic "human in the loop" always fails because it relies on one passive generalist reviewer. Instead, winning companies need "expert-driven loops" with active expert human collaboration. Key principles: name one expert owner who owns consequences when the answer is wrong, require experts to explain decisions rather than blindly approving outputs, and design workflows where specialists communicate with each other while inspecting agent outputs.

- [AI Agents Are Here, But Don't Call Them Boss](https://en-gb.newsroom.workday.com/2025-08-12-New-Workday-Global-Research-AI-Agents-Are-Here,-But-Dont-Call-Them-Boss?asPDF=1) - Workday research showing 75% of employees ready to work with AI agents but not for them

### Three Human Handoffs to Protect

Jordan identifies three critical human handoffs that organizations must protect before AI workflows silently eliminate them:

1. **Judgment** -- Name the human who owns consequences when the answer is wrong
2. **Learning** -- Make people explain decisions instead of blindly approving agent output (active expert collaboration vs. passive review)
3. **Belonging** -- Give coaching, recognition, and unstructured conversation a scheduled place to happen

### Accidental Deskilling and Compression Tax

Jordan introduces two related concepts: "accidental deskilling" (AI does the work so you lose the skill) and "compression tax" (AI speed overloads your brain). He references his earlier Episode 757 ("The Seven Silent Sins of Doing AI Right") which covers the "agent bun sandwich" -- where AI hollows out your core expertise. With agents producing at 20x speed, the compression tax becomes a real cognitive risk: workers accomplish more but retain less.
