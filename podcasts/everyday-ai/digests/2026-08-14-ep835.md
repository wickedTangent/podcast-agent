# Episode 835 -- Jordan Wilson's Nine Most Used AI Tools and Workflows

**Episode Number:** 835

## Topics Covered

### Codex Desktop as the Dominant AI Agent Harness
Jordan Wilson declares OpenAI's Codex desktop agent (the desktop version of ChatGPT, also interchangeable with ChatGPT Work) to be "in a league of its own" among agentic desktop tools. He compares it directly to Claude Code, Cursor, and Antigravity, stating that Claude Code has been "smoked" since February and that no competitor will catch Codex anytime soon. His usage has shifted from 10% desktop in January to 90% desktop today, driven by tools including Codex, Claude Code, Claude Cowork, Cursor, Antigravity, and Gemini desktop. He uses both a Claude 200 Max and an OpenAI 200 Max plan but spends the majority of his time in Codex. He emphasizes that his workflows require full computer access (YOLO mode) which may not be feasible for enterprise users with restricted data policies.
- [Claude Code (Anthropic)](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-code) - Anthropic's coding agent for comparison
- [Cursor (Cursor)](https://cursor.com/) - AI-powered code editor with autonomous agents

### Third Shift Agents: Automated Overnight Stats and Comms Triage
Jordan's most-used AI workflow is not manual use but automated "third shift" agents that work from 11PM to 5AM. His stats agent automates daily podcast metric collection from Buzzsprout (via browser control) and Beehive newsletter analytics (via MCP and browser control), replacing what used to be 30 minutes of manual work with a 30-second briefing. The comms triage agent monitors five different Gmail accounts and LinkedIn messages, triaging incoming pitches, sponsorship requests, and collaborations while providing draft responses. It uses Chrome profiles with extensions (including a LinkedIn message sync extension called "condo") because some tools require Chrome extensions that the built-in Codex browser cannot access. He references episode 806 (desktop agent lingo simplified) for explanations of goals, loops, plans, and sub-agents in Codex and Claude Code.

### ChatGPT Sites as Personal App Builder with iMessage Notifications
Jordan considers ChatGPT Sites (the web-based app builder within ChatGPT, similar to Lovable or Bolt) to be "criminally underrated" for building personal apps. He uses it to create lightweight apps with databases (read/write), light/dark mode support, adjustable fonts, and bookmarking. His agents update these apps automatically throughout the day, and new updates are delivered to his iPhone via iMessage -- a feature he enables through agent instructions that permit texting "Jordan only." He contrasts Sites favorably with Claude Artifacts, noting that Sites offers better permissions (public sharing, forced login via ChatGPT, team sharing) and database capabilities that Artifacts lacks. He describes this as having "agents updating personal apps" without needing to rebuild or maintain them.

### Parrot: Local Dictation App Built with Claude and Codex
Jordan built his own local speech-to-text dictation app called "Parrot" using both Claude and Codex, because cloud-based alternatives like Whisperflow are "too slow" and "not always accurate." Parrot runs entirely locally on his computer with no cloud dependency, resulting in near-zero lag -- critical for his heavy dictation workflow. He uses it daily during screen recordings and livestreams (visible as a green dot at the bottom of his screen). Beyond dictation, Parrot includes a YouTube monitoring feature that automatically transcribes every new video from subscribed channels as they're published. A scheduled Codex skill then reviews these transcriptions daily and delivers a brief of "what everyone on YouTube is talking about," replacing his previous 30-45 minute daily YouTube browsing habit.

### AI App Design: Google Stitch, Claude Artifacts, and GPT Image
For front-end design of vibe-coded apps, Jordan uses a combination of three tools: Claude Artifacts (Anthropic's built-in preview/creation feature), Google Stitch (Google's AI design tool), and GPT Image (OpenAI's image generation). He uploads inspiration images or designs, dictates what he wants to build, and gets different outputs from each. He notes that Claude Artifacts doesn't produce visuals but creates great mockups and slides. Google Stitch excels at mockups, uses nano-banana models, generates images, and can build complete websites. GPT Image handles image generation. He prefers exporting from Google Stitch to Google AI Studio (rather than Claude Artifacts to Claude Code, which he says "just sends HTML, which is not the best") for more flexible downstream use.

### Gemini Notebook for Learning and Content Generation
Google's Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) is used almost daily by Jordan, sometimes for multiple hours. It generates audio overviews, custom slide decks, reports, and infographics based on user-sourced documents. He uses it for episode planning by having Codex pull signals from Twitter bookmarks, newsletter click-through rates from Beehive, and trending topics, then create custom Gemini Notebooks from that research. His favorite feature is the audio overview with interactive mode -- he can "join" the two-host audio deep dive and ask questions in real-time, effectively using it as a rehearsal tool before major interviews. He describes it as "not just a content generation machine" but "really interactive learning," noting that sometimes he's learning at the same rate as his audience when new AI technologies emerge.
- [Gemini Notebook (Google)](https://notebook.google/) - Official product page

### Codex Skill Building: 124 Skills, 6,179 Uses
Jordan measures AI proficiency not by lines of code or tokens used but by "the number of times that you use a skill." He has created 124 skills in Codex that he has used a total of 6,179 times (from a single account). His approach to skills is systematic: break multi-system, multi-app, multi-website tasks into individual steps and save each as a skill. Multiple skills can be combined into plugins in Codex. He creates skills frequently -- any task he performs by hand that he might repeat becomes a candidate for skill creation. He even has a daily skill that scans all his Codex threads and suggests new skills based on repeated patterns (e.g., "you went back and forth six or seven times, this should be a skill"). He emphasizes that the resulting skills are "your IP" and "your knowledge," and urges listeners to "stop being a button pusher" and turn repeated manual work into reusable skills.

### ChatGPT Voice Remote: Full-Duplex Voice Computer Control
Jordan describes ChatGPT Voice in remote mode as "insanely good" and "the next frontier of agentic work." To access it: connect the ChatGPT app to Codex or ChatGPT Work desktop once, then open ChatGPT on the mobile app, go to the side panel, click Remote, and tap the blue "ChatGPT Live" button. Unlike the standard ChatGPT voice mode (which cannot access connectors or MCPs), remote voice mode has full access to the computer, connectors, and MCPs. It uses full-duplex communication -- you can talk to it, interrupt it, and it can interrupt you, while a background model processes your commands. He describes the experience as having "an intern sitting in front of my computer" and uses it while walking to get work done outdoors. He contrasts it with Cursor Dispatch, which he calls "terrible" because it "only can work on one thread and can't really do anything."
